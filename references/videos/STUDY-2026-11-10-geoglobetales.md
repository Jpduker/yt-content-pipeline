## Look
- **Base = satellite imagery of the real Earth** (Blue-Marble-style, dark blue oceans, green/brown land, slight relief), 9:16, always top-down
  or a gently tilted globe. No characters on screen (sometimes a small round portrait pin of the person in the story).
- **Graphics on the map:** bright yellow route lines that draw on with a glow, neon-green glowing region fills (outline + bloom), red danger
  overlays with a skull, red/white circular icon badges (no-entry, soldier, trees, crossed-out symbols), pins, dotted paths, distance labels
  written along the line ("131 km", "80 km", "-50°C"), big numbers in 3D-ish white type ("27 years", "1998", "2 years", "11 years"),
  country fill in flat red with its name, a passport + visa stamp prop, a "FAILED" stamp on a parchment map, a 3D Earth with a stat card
  ("25 countries · 47,000 km").
- Occasional real stock footage inserts (misty rainforest, mountains) for 2–4 s.

## Camera + pacing
- Constant slow drift/zoom on the map (push in to a region, pull out to the continent, pan along a route). A new graphic or a move every 2–3 s;
  a full re-frame every 4–6 s. Route lines are the "character" that travels.

## Captions + audio
- Captions are tiny: one short phrase (2–4 words) in small white sans at ~70% height, no box, low contrast; the visuals carry the meaning.
- Calm male narration, documentary tone; light ambient music under it. (Not measured.)

## Story + titles
- "Why…?" / "The difference between…" / "The strangest border…" / "Most dangerous place…" — one curious geography fact per Short, 45–100 s.
- Structure: hook question on a map → the surprising fact → 3–4 reasons with graphics → closing number/stat card.
- Titles: question or superlative + 1–3 emojis + the proper noun ("Why Chile Is So Long & So Thin 🇨🇱🌶️ History of the Longest & Narrowest Country 🤯").

## What we could take (if we ever do a map/geo line)
Satellite base + glowing route lines + neon region fills + icon badges + numbers along the path; small captions; 60–100 s vertical facts.
