# WI11 script: "What If Everyone You Ignored Slowly Faded Away?" (v1, 2026-11-13, approved idea)
Narration: second person, present tense; observational and dry, then sudden and close.
Tags: N narrator · YOU hero · BARISTA · BOSS · COLL (Dana) · THEO · X (the man from the lift).
Rule (all stated by 0:30, never changed): the people you don't pay attention to fade, a little more each day you ignore them, until you can see through them. Real attention (looking up, listening, asking) brings them back. Nobody else sees it; the fading is only in your eyes. If someone fades all the way, they drift out of your life: you just stop noticing they've gone.
Motif: translucent figures (alpha by "days ignored"); your phone glow as the thing you look at instead.

## HOOK
> cold open: the café counter, Friday. The barista is almost see-through; you can read the menu through his chest.
N: By Friday, you can see through the barista.
N: Not a little. You can read the menu through his chest.
~ 0.4
N: He's been making your coffee for two years. You've never asked his name.
~ 0.5
N: And he's not the only one.

## ACT 1
> Monday. Office lift. A man you see every day, a little faint.
N: It starts on Monday. The man in the lift is a bit pale. You assume it's the lighting.
> office: Dana, slightly faded, talking to you while you type.
N: On Tuesday, Dana is talking to you while you answer an email. Halfway through her sentence, you notice you can see the printer behind her.
COLL: …so that's my news. What do you think?
YOU: Great! Yeah. Great.
COLL: I said my cat died.
~ 0.6
N: She fades a little more. You watch it happen.
YOU: Dana. Sorry. I wasn't listening. Tell me about your cat.
> she tells you. Her colour comes back while she talks.
N: She tells you. The cat was called Biscuit, and he hated everyone except her. With every sentence, she gets a little more solid.
N: So that's the rule. Ignore someone, and they fade. Pay attention, and they come back.

## ACT 2
> a montage: you looking up from your phone at people.
N: You get good at it. You learn the barista's name: Kofi. You ask the lift man about his dog. You stop typing when your boss talks, which surprises him more than anyone.
BOSS: Are you… listening?
YOU: Yes.
BOSS: Nobody listens. This is very unsettling.
N: By Thursday, the city looks solid again. You're exhausted. Paying attention, it turns out, is a full-time job.
> Friday night. The flat. You on your phone.
N: On Friday night, you get home, drop onto the sofa, and open your phone.
THEO: Did you see the score?
YOU: Mm.
THEO: I said, did you see the—
~ 0.8
> you look up. Theo is almost completely transparent. You can see the sofa cushions through him.
N: You look up.
~ 0.6
N: You can see the cushions through your brother.
N: Not a little. Almost all the way.
~ 0.6
N: You checked everyone. The barista, the lift, the boss. You never checked the person on your sofa.

## ACT 3
YOU: Theo. What score?
THEO: Doesn't matter.
YOU: It does. Who played?
THEO: You hate football.
YOU: I know. Tell me anyway.
> he tells you. Slowly, then all at once.
N: He tells you. All of it. The match, the referee, a goal he's still angry about. Then, somehow, his job interview on Monday, which you didn't know about.
YOU: You've got an interview?
THEO: I told you. Twice.
YOU: Tell me a third time. I'm listening now.
> his outline sharpens; his colour floods back. He's solid.
N: By midnight, he's solid again. Fully, annoyingly solid. He's eaten all your crisps.
> Saturday morning, café. The barista: fully there.
BARISTA: Morning! The usual?
YOU: Morning, Kofi. How's your weekend?
N: Kofi looks so surprised you think he might drop the cup.
N: He doesn't fade. Neither does anyone else. Not this week.

## END

N (soft): Who would be fading around you right now?
