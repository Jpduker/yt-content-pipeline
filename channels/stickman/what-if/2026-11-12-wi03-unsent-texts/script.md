# WI03 script: "What If You Could See Every Text People Almost Sent You?" (v1, 2026-11-11)
Narration: second person, present tense, confessional and quiet; the narrator steps back once a scene starts (Vivek, 2026-11-11).
Tags: N narrator · YOU hero (spoken) · MIRA · BOSS · COLL · MUM · PIZZA · JO, SAM (group chat) · LIFT (stranger). `-T` = an UNSENT text, read in the sender's voice (soft, grey ghost bubble).
`(text)` = a real, sent message. `~ 0.8` = extra pause in seconds before the next line. `>` = stage direction (not spoken).

## HOOK
N: You wake up, and your phone is full of messages nobody sent.
N: Your boss typed,
BOSS-T: You're fired.
N: Then deleted it.
N: Your mum typed,
MUM-T: Are you eating properly?
N: Eleven times.
~ 0.35
N: And at three a.m., your best friend typed three words… and held backspace.
~ 0.55
MIRA-T: I'm not okay.
~ 0.5
N: She never sent it.
N: You could answer anyway.

## ACT 1
> bedroom, phone glowing; grey bubbles pile up; counter UNSENT 1 → 212
N: They look like normal messages. Just grey. And they flicker, like they're still deciding.
N: Everything anyone almost said to you since midnight.
N: Two hundred and twelve. Before breakfast.
N: The pizza place wrote,
PIZZA-T: Sorry about the pineapple.
N: Then sent a coupon instead.
~ 0.3
N: The group chat is worse.
JO-T: Is he bringing the guitar again?
SAM-T: Somebody tell him. Not me.
~ 0.4
N: Nobody tells you. Tonight, the guitar stays home.
> lift. A stranger glances at the hoodie, types, deletes.
N: In the lift, a stranger types,
LIFT-T: I like your hoodie.
N: And deletes it.
~ 0.5
N: You stand a little taller anyway.

## ACT 2
> office. A grey bubble hangs over the boss's phone.
N: At work, it turns out to be useful.
BOSS-T: Where is that report?
YOU: Report's on your desk.
BOSS: I was… just about to ask.
YOU: Lucky guess.
> colleague types, deletes, types, deletes
COLL-T: Could you cover Friday? No. Forget it.
YOU: I can cover Friday.
COLL: How did you—
YOU: You look like a Friday.
N: By lunch, you're answering questions nobody asked.
N: It feels like kindness.
BOSS-T: Should I warn him about the budget?
YOU: Is something wrong with the budget?
BOSS: Who told you that?
~ 0.4
N: It doesn't feel like kindness to them.
N: By three, people turn their phones face down when you walk past.
~ 0.6
> his phone: the mum bubble again. The counter ticks 11 → 12.
MUM-T: Are you eating properly?
N: Twelve.
N: So you call her.
MUM (phone): Hello? Is something wrong?
YOU: No, no. I just had lunch. A real one. Vegetables and everything.
MUM (phone): Why are you telling me this?
YOU: Thought you'd want to know.
~ 0.5
MUM (phone): I did. I did want to know.
N: That one, you got right. You answered. And she never knew you'd read it.

## ACT 3
N: All afternoon, one message sits at the top of the pile. You don't open it. You don't need to.
> café, evening. Mira arrives smiling too hard.
MIRA: Hey, you.
YOU: Hey. How are you?
MIRA: Fine! Tired. You know. Fine.
MIRA: How was work?
YOU: Weird. People kept hiding their phones.
MIRA: Maybe stop reading over their shoulders.
~ 0.5
YOU: Yeah. Maybe.
~ 0.4
N: Above her phone. Grey. Still flickering.
MIRA-T: I'm not okay.
N: You've had the perfect reply ready all day.
N: You type it under the table.
YOU-T: I know you're not okay.
N: Your thumb is on send.
~ 0.6
N: She didn't send it. That was a decision. Hers.
~ 0.5
N: You hold backspace.
N: Now there are two unsent messages at this table.
~ 0.6
YOU: Want to split a cinnamon roll?
MIRA: Yeah. Okay.
N: You talk about nothing. Twenty minutes of nothing.
~ 0.7
MIRA: Can I tell you something?
YOU: Always.
~ 0.8
MIRA: I'm not okay.
~ 0.7
N: Three words. Out loud. Sixteen hours late, and exactly on time.
YOU: I'm here.
~ 0.5
MIRA: I didn't get it. The second interview. I've been saying fine for a week.
YOU: That's a long time to say fine.
MIRA: It's a very flexible word.
~ 0.6
N: You don't say you knew.
~ 0.9
> walking home, phone buzzes: a blue, solid bubble
N: On the way home, your phone buzzes. A real one, this time.
MIRA (text): Thank you for not asking.
