# Narrative playbook

Current story gate: see STORY_QUALITY_STANDARD.md. The user reports strong visuals but an underwhelming pilot plot to a GTA gamer. New production prioritizes concrete crime stakes, practical obstacle logic, character agency and planted payoffs; visual polish alone does not pass editorial review.

A conversation between characters is a delivery choice, not a timeline or POV by itself. These axes can be combined.

| Axis | Choices | Useful rule for Shorts |
|---|---|---|
| Timeline | Linear, nonlinear, parallel, episodic | Default to one clear causal sequence; one purposeful flashback can create mystery |
| POV | First person, second person, third-person limited, omniscient | Decide whose knowledge the viewer is allowed to have |
| Delivery | Narrator-led, character dialogue, hybrid | Pick the form that makes the central choice feel immediate |
| Story focus | Heist, betrayal, pursuit, loyalty, mystery, escape | One central dilemma, with a consequence and payoff |
| Series relation | Standalone, recurring setting, multi-part arc | Each upload needs a satisfying beat even when an arc continues |

## Recommended formats
1. Narrator-led crime micro-story: Liam is the default; use Laura or Lara as the story requires. Good for setups and cause/effect across locations.
2. Dialogue-led confrontation: let Jason and Lucia discover a problem through a short exchange. Every line must reveal a clue, change a choice, or raise stakes. Establish who speaks with framing and restrained speaker labels.
3. First-person confession: a character tells what they did or concealed. Good for intimacy and unreliable narration.
4. Hybrid: a one-sentence hook, a character exchange at the turning point, and a brief narrated consequence.
5. Nonlinear reveal: open on the consequence, use one clear return to the earlier choice, then resolve the initial image.

## Static-image dialogue
Match the speaking character to the frame. Keep exchanges brief. Use a reaction shot for a meaningful beat. Do not imply generated mouths are synchronized when the pictures are still.
Avoid an unexplained fourth voice. Limit the cast to two speaking characters in most 30–45-second dialogue tests.
Use sound perspective for an actual phone character voice; keep a third-person narrator dry and centered.
