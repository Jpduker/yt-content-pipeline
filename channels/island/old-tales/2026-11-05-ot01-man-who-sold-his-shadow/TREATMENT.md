# Old Tales of the Island #1: "The Man Who Sold His Shadow" (ukiyo-e, dark fable Short, ~55 s)

Original story (no folk tale retold). One constant subject: the fisherman's SHADOW. The story is told by where the
shadow sits in each print: long on the sand → peeled off and rolled up → missing at noon → standing on the water →
walking into the sea → two shadows on an empty bay.

Three structures considered:
1. Alternate impressions of one shore print (dusk / night / noon / night): strong, but the bargain needs close-ups.
2. A triptych revealed sheet by sheet: the ending (two shadows) only reads when all three join. Too slow for 55 s.
3. **The handscroll (chosen)**: prints slide right→left on a long mount; each print holds one small action
   (8 fps figures, camera on ones). Shorter slides as the story speeds up toward the sea. Ending: the last
   print washes back to paper, the red seal remains, then the scroll rolls back to print 1 (seamless loop).

Prints: 1 shore at dusk (hook line as the cartouche) · 2 forty tally strokes on a post, empty nets ·
3 night, the mist woman rises (paper-white figure in mist bands) · 4 the bargain: the shadow peels off the sand
and rolls up like paper · 5 noon, nets overflowing, no shadow under him · 6 village street: whispers, a wife turns
away · 7 night, he rows out · 8 the shadow given back stands on the water and walks into the sea; he follows ·
9 still bay under a moon: two shadows cross the water, nobody casting them.
Pigments: Prussian blue family for sea/sky, pale blue, ochre sand, grey-green, sumi; beni red only for the seal and
the mist woman's lips. Sound: shakuhachi breath phrases, shamisen pulse that drops out at the bargain, taiko
heartbeat at the sea, hyōshigi to open, absolute silence (ma) before the last line.
