**Patterns (what wins):**
1. **A rule or an impossible fact in the first line** ("In this town, X is deadly", "The ground is a lie", "Every night at 3:12…"). Our hooks already follow this; keep them under 12 words.
2. **Viewer participation** beats passive watching: doors to choose, "who escapes first", "can you solve it". → our closing question is right; add a **"pick one" frame** once a week (3 suspects, 3 doors).
3. **Animals carry story Shorts** (Mystic Cat Stories outlier 23.7; TVadventure; Animated Stories). → Mabel/Pip/Rollo should get their own POV episodes.
4. **Shorts pay ~$0.10/1K views; long-form mystery/detective pays $8–14 RPM.** Shorts grow the audience, long-form earns. No pure-Shorts story channel in this sample earns meaningfully from Shorts alone.
5. **Look matters less than hook + clarity** — the top earners are often plain AI images. Our 2.5D look is a *differentiator*, not a requirement; it wins on thumbnail/first-frame recognition and binge loyalty, not on raw reach.
6. **No pixel-art/diorama mystery serial showed up in the data.** That's the gap: nobody combines a recognisable toy world + serial mystery + puzzle participation.

## 2. Channel identity
**Name (recommended): The Keeper's Island** — searchable ("keeper", "island"), serial-friendly, works as a brand for spin-offs. Alternates: *Lamplight Isle*, *Twelve Seconds of Dark* (too chapter-specific), *The Island Files* (more investigative).
**Handle:** @KeepersIsland (check availability; fallback @TheKeepersIsland).
**Promise (one line, use everywhere):** *One island. One lighthouse. A new clue every day.*
**Motive:** a cozy-but-tense investigative serial where the audience solves alongside the keeper; every clue is on screen before it matters (fair-play mystery), so comments become the detective room.
**Audience:** 13–35, general (NOT made for kids), fans of cozy mysteries, pixel-art games, detective puzzles, folklore.

## 3. The investigative world (bible, short form)
- **Core mystery (season):** something under the island signals to the sea through the lamp; it is tied to the night Gus was born and to the "man who sold his shadow" legend.
- **Fair-play rule:** every answer is visible earlier (prints, clock, cables, marks, logbook).
- **Cast:** Gus (keeper, lantern), Mabel (cat, solver), Pip (gull, aerial witness), Rollo (mouse, comic + brave), Old Wren (postmistress, lore), Dunn (harbour master, antagonist → reluctant ally), the Stranger (yellow coat, no shadow).
- **Places that grow:** lighthouse + lamp room, village + post office, harbour office, cliff path + cottage, north rocks, the door in the rock → **week 2: the tunnel and the sea-cave** (new set).
- **Time of day as tone:** day = investigation and humour; dusk = lore; night/storm = dread and cliffhangers.

## 5. One style or several?
**Recommendation: keep 2.5D pixel as the ONLY style for this channel's main world.** Brand recognition in the feed is the whole value of a distinctive look; mixing styles makes the channel look like a compilation and confuses the algorithm's audience match.
**One exception that helps:** *Old Tales of the Island* may use a **second, clearly "storybook" look** (e.g. lemo-opuscar's ukiyo-e or shadow-puppet) because they are legends told inside the world — framed as "the tale Wren tells", it reads as intentional (like a flashback), and it reuses our OT01 work. Keep it to that one series.
**Other styles → other channels**, as already planned (Between the Ifs = stickman; Track B = kids look; Whispers = poetic). Different style = different audience = different channel.

## 6. Launch checklist
1. Create channel, name/handle, description, banner, avatar (above). Audience: not made for kids.
2. Upload S01 + S02 Monday; schedule the rest (2/day) once rendered.
3. Pin the question comment on each; reply to the first theories (comments drive Shorts distribution).
4. After 48 h: check swipe-away % per Short (YouTube Studio → Shorts "viewed vs swiped away"); <60 % viewed = hook problem → we fix the first 2 s for week 2.
5. Week 2: first long-form compilation of Chapter 1 + one "Pick One" Short.
